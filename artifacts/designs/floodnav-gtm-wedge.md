## Problem Statement

FloodNav has a fully-specified 12-month target architecture (`artifacts/SW Architecture.md`): dual-horizon 3Di simulation, gauge hot-start, citizen app, city council dashboard, FCM/APNs alerting, Phase-3 partner API. It is not what's currently being built — a prior CEO/eng review already cut a much narrower Phase 1 (single 3Di slot, no gauge access, raster-only depth source, single 2h cycle, citizen floodmap + routing only). This session pressure-tested the *product* reasoning behind continuing to build toward the full platform, given the current commercial reality: one active pilot relationship (GreenSM, a Vietnamese taxi/ride-hailing provider) with infrastructure committed but no data shipped yet, and no government relationship started.

## Demand Evidence

- GreenSM has "expressed immense interest" and the relationship is framed internally as "in pilot" — but on inspection, the only concrete artifact is an S3 bucket GreenSM provisioned to receive data. **Zero data has flowed.** Interest and infra-provisioning are real signals (GreenSM spent engineering effort), but they are not usage.
- Named champion identified: **Phan Ngọc Luân, Senior Product Manager** ("Chuyên viên Quản lý sản phẩm cao cấp") at GreenSM. This closes the "no named owner" gap from the initial diagnostic.
- Government relationship exists: FloodNav's team is working with **NAWAPI**, piloting a digital-twin water-management project for the **Cai River in Nha Trang**. This is real, not a thesis — but it is a *different* river, city, and (per the pilot description) a different product framing (digital twin / water management) than FloodNav's Hanoi flood-navigation platform. It's evidence of government engagement capability and domain credibility in Vietnam's water-management space; it is not yet evidence that Hanoi city council specifically wants FloodNav's dashboard. Worth distinguishing "we can work with Vietnamese government water agencies" (now evidenced) from "Hanoi wants this" (still unconfirmed).

## Status Quo

GreenSM's stated status quo for flooded roads is "nothing — they just eat the losses." Concretely, per the domain owner: a flooded road can strand a vehicle, requiring the driver to be physically carried out of the car, and the vehicle then has to go to a GSM-owned facility for repair. This is a real, specific, non-hypothetical failure mode — but it is currently **unquantified**: no incident count, no dollar figure, no frequency-per-flood-event number exists from GreenSM or from FloodNav's own estimate. The only cited evidence so far was a national-level Reuters figure on Typhoon Bualoi property damage (~$303M), which is a macro disaster statistic, not GreenSM's specific cost — it establishes that Vietnam has a flooding problem, not that this problem is expensive for this customer.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

Two distinct buyer segments emerged during the session, matching the split already implicit in the architecture doc:
- **Corporate/fleet customers (GreenSM)** — want the **API data feed** (segment-level passability, near-real-time). This is the near-term commercial priority; GreenSM is the only party with committed infrastructure today.
- **Government/city council** — wants the **dashboard** (situational awareness, dispatch logging, pre-closure alerts). No relationship exists yet; this buyer is currently a thesis, not evidence.

The narrowest validated wedge, given actual current commitments, is: a validated, sub-hourly, road-segment passability feed delivered to GreenSM's existing S3 bucket — not the full-city dual-horizon platform.

## Constraints

- Only one 3Di simulation slot — no dual-horizon (constant_cycle + background_cycle) concurrency as originally specified in the target architecture.
- No gauge/sensor access — Tomorrow.io rainfall forecast is the only live input; gauge hot-start is not buildable in Phase 1.
- Depth source of truth is raster (GeoTIFF/XYZ), not the target doc's vector/GeoJSON/PostGIS-segment model.
- Full Phase 1 engineering scope (T1–T18) is already CEO- and eng-reviewed and documented in `artifacts/[20260904] FloodNav_Phase1_Consolidated_Plan.md`.

## Premises

1. The core differentiating wedge is sub-hourly, road-segment-level passability feeding pre-route decisions — not a general flood-risk score. Generic flood-risk APIs (Ambee, National Flood Data, Precisely) are property/insurance-grade, static/daily flood-zone data; they do not solve "is this road passable in the next 30 minutes." (Agreed.)
2. GreenSM's pain is both traffic-jam disruption and driver/vehicle safety incidents (stranded vehicle, driver carried out, vehicle repair) — still unquantified in dollars or incident counts. (Agreed, refined during session — traffic disruption itself was added as a named pain alongside stranded-vehicle incidents.)
3. Buyer segmentation is real: corporate customers need the API feed; government customers need the dashboard. The full SW Architecture.md platform matters for the government segment eventually, but GreenSM — the only party with committed infrastructure today — needs the feed, not the dashboard. (Agreed, with an explicit founder decision below that diverges from the recommended sequencing.)

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Ship validated feed ASAP (minimal viable)
Finish only what's required to validate the raster-sampling regression check (T5/T9 — a diff of the new sampling pipeline's per-road depths against archived known-good cycles) and ship the existing Phase 1 data feed to GreenSM's S3 bucket for their coverage area, even at current accuracy. Fastest path to real usage and real incident data. Risk: shipping unvalidated depth data into a live fleet-safety context is not a pure accuracy question — a bad routing call around a flood is a safety issue. **Note:** this approach assumes the data is close to shippable; see the shippability contradiction in Open Questions below, which this approach depends on resolving first.

### Approach B: Instrument the pain before shipping more pipeline
Before finishing T5–T9, get GreenSM to share an incident log (stranded vehicles, repairs, driver time lost) for recent flood events, to quantify Premise #2 and pick the highest-value geographic wedge. Directly closes the biggest evidence gap from this session. Risk: doesn't ship product this cycle; GreenSM's incident data may not exist in usable form.

### Approach C: Narrow geographic wedge, hand-validated (creative/lateral)
Hand-validate only the 3–5 corridors/districts GreenSM cares most about to a high confidence bar, ship that narrow slice now, expand coverage after the integration itself is proven. Matches "narrow beats wide, early." Risk: manual validation doesn't scale and may conflict with T5's automated regression approach; gaps outside the hand-picked corridors are exposed exactly when a flood event needs full coverage.

## Recommended Approach

**Session recommendation was Approach A or C (ship or hand-validate a narrow wedge to GreenSM now).** The user explicitly overrode this: **decision is to continue building the full pipeline (current Phase 1 scope, T1–T18) and ship to GreenSM once the whole pipeline — not just GreenSM's corridors — is validated.**

Stated rationale: at full-city or country scale, only a government-funded project can sustain the platform, so the full pipeline is the real target regardless of GreenSM's near-term timeline. Sequencing, per the founder: complete Phase 1 (see Phase 1 Plan Summary below), then GreenSM ships.

**This diverges from the recommended approach — though the counter-argument is weaker than it was at the time it was made.** At the time of the pushback, "government funding justifies the full build" looked like an unevidenced thesis. It's since surfaced that a real government relationship exists (NAWAPI, Cai River digital twin) — so the claim "we can execute government-funded water-management projects" is now evidenced, not hypothetical. What's *not* yet evidenced is that this specific relationship (different river, different city, different product framing) transfers into Hanoi city council wanting FloodNav's dashboard specifically. The residual risk stands: GreenSM has a provisioned integration point and a named champion today; the Hanoi government relationship for *this* product does not yet exist. Building the full pipeline first still risks the GreenSM relationship cooling while a Hanoi-specific government motion — which is not the same motion as the NAWAPI/Nha Trang one — has not yet started. The user held this position after the pushback; it is recorded here as a decision made with better information than the first pass, not a resolved disagreement.

## Phase 1 Plan Summary (Consolidated)

The full backend/frontend scope for "the full pipeline" the founder chose to build first is already CEO- and eng-reviewed, at `artifacts/[20260904] FloodNav_Phase1_Consolidated_Plan.md`. Summarized here so the sequencing decision above is legible without opening a second document:

**Real infra constraints that shrink Phase 1 relative to SW Architecture.md:** only one 3Di simulation slot (no dual-horizon), no gauge/sensor access (Tomorrow.io rain forecast only), raster (not vector/GeoJSON) as depth source of truth, single continuous 2h cycle, citizen floodmap + routing only (dashboard/mobile/alerting deferred to Phase 2/3).

**Accepted Phase 1 scope:**
- Depth pipeline rebuilt around raster (GeoTIFF/XYZ tiles); routing cost sampled via `rasterio` against road segments, 5m road densification, max-valid-depth per road per timestep (`sample_road_depths.py`), replacing the old GeoJSON extract/merge/mapping path.
- PostGIS pre-sampling of per-segment depth once per cycle (transactional UPSERT, not delete+insert) — this is what makes route/point queries fast enough for real citizen or partner load.
- TimescaleDB stood up now for depth history — no longer zero-consumer, since the frontend's depth-forecast chart reads it directly.
- Out-of-extent roads (zero valid raster samples) are hard-excluded from routing, never soft-penalized — absence of data must never look like confirmation of safety.
- `floodmap-backend` (port 8010) rewritten (not extended) to read PostGIS instead of polling the newest MinIO GeoJSON object.
- Error handling for 3Di auth-token expiry (401/403), corrupt/undersized raster detection, and distinguishing a failed simulation from a legitimate no-rain skip in the Airflow UI.

**Not in Phase 1 scope:** gauge ingestion/hot-start (blocked on sensor access), dual-horizon/background_cycle (blocked on single-3Di-slot constraint), institution/route histograms, city council dashboard, mobile app, FCM/APNs alerting, Phase-3 partner API, rate limiting (deferred pre-launch).

**Task list:** 18 backend tasks (T4–T18, T1–T3 already resolved/dropped) plus 2 eng-review findings (E1–E2), sequenced with dependencies — see the source doc for per-task effort estimates, file paths, and verification steps. The mandatory regression gate before the old GeoJSON path can be deleted is T9 (diff new per-road depths against archived cycles within tolerance).

**How this connects to the GreenSM decision above:** the founder's stated trigger for shipping to GreenSM is "after Phase 1" — i.e., after this task list (particularly T5/T6/T9, the raster pipeline + PostGIS + regression validation) is complete, not after a GreenSM-specific subset is validated. T7 (TimescaleDB) and T16–T18 (vehicle costing profiles, tile serving, draining flag) are not obviously required to satisfy GreenSM's API-feed need specifically — worth revisiting whether all 18 tasks gate the GreenSM ship date, or only a subset, when Success Criteria's "whole pipeline validated" bar gets defined.

## Open Questions

- **Resolved — shippability:** data is *partially* validated. The remaining gap isn't a validation-process gap, it's a **model-drift risk**: Hanoi has implemented unspecified hardware flood-mitigation infrastructure, and FloodNav has no information on its specs. The simulation/depth model needs re-validation against real 2026 rain and flood data to check whether the city's own mitigation changes have shifted ground truth out from under the model's calibration. This is a bigger deal than a checklist item — it means the model could be confidently wrong in a way that's invisible until compared against fresh real-world data, in exactly the areas GreenSM would route through. Recommend making this an explicit Phase 1 exit gate, not a background task.
- **Resolved — named champion:** Phan Ngọc Luân, Senior Product Manager, GreenSM.
- **Resolved — government relationship:** exists (NAWAPI, Cai River digital twin, Nha Trang) but doesn't directly validate Hanoi/FloodNav dashboard demand — see Demand Evidence above for the distinction.
- **Still open:** what does a flooded-road incident actually cost GreenSM, in dollars or incident counts? Not yet quantified by either party. This is now the single biggest remaining evidence gap in the whole pitch.
- **New:** what, specifically, has Hanoi installed as flood-mitigation hardware, and can FloodNav get any information on it (even indirectly, via city contacts)? Currently zero visibility, and it directly affects model accuracy.
